A catalogue of quadruples

Every region of Schmidt's subdivision carries a Descartes quadruple, and there are two ways it does so. A circular region together with its three circular children is one; a triangular region's three sides together with the circle inscribed in it is the other. Both hold at every generation without exception, so the number of quadruples is exactly the number of regions. Generation 0 is a fair check on that: 𝒥 with its three circular children is (0, 0, 2, 2) — the strip.

Three restrictions, and two of them are free. Occurrences are counted in the first strip only — the arrangement is periodic with period 1, so a region outside 0 ≤ x < 1 is a translate of one inside and carries the very same four numbers. The strip holds exactly 5ⁿ of the (3·5ⁿ − 1)/2 regions and loses nothing: all 831 quadruples occur in it. And primitive costs nothing either, since every quadruple here has factor exactly 2 and dividing it out merges no rows.

The third restriction is real. The seven children of 𝒥 give only three distinct sets of quadruples, because they duplicate: 𝒱₂ and 𝒱₃ are the two circles and are translates of one another, ℰ₁ and C are the middle pieces, 𝒱₁ and ℰ₂ and ℰ₃ the outer ones. To generation 5 the circles give 512 and the middle 318 — and those two are disjoint, so together with the seed's own (0, 0, 1, 1) they are the entire catalogue. The outer 177 are contained in them and add nothing at all. So from defaults to the two circles, which is the family worth reading, and widening it costs nothing but rows.

Bends are shown primitive by default; unticking shows them as the arrangement carries them, which is twice the primitive form — every curvature here is even, so every quadruple has a factor of 2 to divide out. Lowest terms shows the primitive names instead. Following a row always opens the primitive quadruple in the designer either way, because that is the one that can be placed: a packing is put on the Gaussian integers in lowest terms, and asking for (−2, 4, 4, 6) is correctly refused where (−1, 2, 2, 3) — the same packing at twice the size — is not. Of the 831 quadruples here, 830 place; the one that does not is (−137, 282, 283, 330), which falls under the placement gap the packings lab already records. Checked once offline rather than on load, since trying all 831 takes 25 seconds.

By generation

Watch distinct here against new here. On the two circles they are the same number at every generation — 4 and 4, 18 and 18, 84 and 84, 405 and 405 — which says that no quadruple ever recurs: each generation contributes only quadruples never seen before. That is the exclusivity the page was built to test, and it holds.

Switch from to everything and it stops holding: 10 distinct at generation 2 of which 7 are new, 40 and 30, 177 and 137. So the restrictions are not tidying — they are the conditions under which the claim is true. Drop any of them and the same four numbers start turning up twice.
