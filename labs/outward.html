The experiment

Click any circle to turn the quadruple inside out through it. That circle stays where it is and swaps its inside for its outside; the other three land within it.

This is not the Vieta jump. Vieta keeps three circles and replaces one, going inward forever. This keeps one and moves three, and goes outward. It is an involution — click the same circle twice and you are back, which is why the circle you arrived through is drawn dashed.

That matters most at the strip, whose two lines look alike. Reflecting through the lower line moves it down, the upper line moves it up; click the same one twice and it flips back and forth. Alternate them and the parallel stack develops as you would expect.

From (−1, 2, 2, 3), the three distinct results are the strip (0, 0, 1, 1), (−2, 3, 6, 7) and (−3, 5, 8, 8). Note the last is not 3 × (−1, 2, 2, 3), which would be the imprimitive (−3, 6, 6, 9).

The strip is the waist. Reflect it through a line and you get another strip; through a circle and you drop back into a bounded packing. Inward is the familiar Apollonian recursion; outward is this.

Exact throughout: the reflection is X ↦ X + 2⟨X,C⟩·C, linear in the augmented coordinates, so it never leaves ℤ × ℤ[i].
