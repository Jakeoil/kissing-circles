Schmidt's subdivision

Every region is subdivided at each generation. There are two kinds, and they behave differently — this is Schmidt's Fig. 2 and Fig. 2*:

circular region→ 7 parts: 3 circular (V₁ V₂ V₃), 4 triangular (E₁ E₂ E₃ C)
triangular region→ 4 parts: 3 triangular (V₁ V₂ V₃), 1 circular (C)

Generation 1 is the unit square: the lines y=0, y=1, x=0, x=1, with a circle of radius ½ at the midpoint of each side. That is two strip packings superimposed — one horizontal, one vertical — at curvature 2 rather than 1, because Schmidt's normalization makes every curvature even.

Why outlines. These circles nest, unlike an Apollonian packing whose interiors are disjoint. Filled, the picture becomes a solid slab with no structure visible. Switch draw to filled to see it happen.

Region counts follow (3·5ⁿ − 1)/2 — 1, 7, 37, 187, 937, 4687 — against the gasket's 4·3ⁿ⁻¹.
