The experiment

Regions of 𝒥 draws the partition at one generation. Every point of the half plane belongs to exactly one region, so this is a single coherent picture, and stepping the generation shows the subdivision happening.

Regions of 𝒥* is the same construction started from Schmidt's other seed: not the half plane but the curvilinear triangle over [0,1], bounded by x = 0, x = 1 and the semicircle between them. A chain is regular when it starts at 𝒥 and dually regular when it starts at 𝒥*, and the algorithm needs both, so the lab should show both.

Same generators, same rules — only the region they act on differs. Neither is a sub-picture of the other, because the rules swap the types: a circular region makes four triangles and a triangular one makes a circle, so after one step each partition contains both. The counts are the same shape with a different constant, (3·5ⁿ − 1)/2 against (3·5ⁿ + 1)/4 — 1, 7, 37, 187, 937 against 1, 4, 19, 94, 469 — and the two are locked together exactly: at every generation 𝒥 has as many circular regions as 𝒥* has triangular ones, and four times as many triangular regions as 𝒥* has circular ones. Checked to generation 6.

Circles draws each region's circumscribed circle instead, for every generation at once — the Schmidt arrangement as it is usually published. Know what that superimposes: for a circular region the circumscribed circle is its boundary; for a triangular region it passes through the three vertices and is not one of its sides. Two different kinds of thing in one picture. Worth seeing, but it is not the partition, and the two should not be read together.

Curvatures numbers the circular regions with their bend — Schmidt's ρ(F). Every one is even, which is his ρ(F) ∈ 2ℕ₀, and the reason the Apollonian gasket sits inside this arrangement at exactly twice the curvature: (0, 0, 1, 1) appears here as (0, 0, 2, 2).

Gasket overlay draws that claim instead of asserting it. The strip packing (0, 0, 1, 1) goes over the picture in gold at Schmidt's normalisation, (0, 0, 2, 2): the lines y = 0 and y = 1, and circles of radius ½ at (0, ½) and (1, ½) — which are 𝒥's own children 𝒱₂ and 𝒱₃. Nothing is fitted or centred; it lands on the arrangement's coordinates as it is, and a packing that had to be moved to line up would not be the claim. Every bend in it is even, as ρ(F) ∈ 2ℕ₀ requires, and every circle up to bend 100 is one of the arrangement's own — as far as a generation-8 sample can confirm. In the region view the gold falls on the boundaries between circular and triangular regions, which is the point: those came out of a subdivision, not out of any Descartes recursion, and they coincide anyway.

Four are offered. Two are the same quadruple (0, 0, 1, 1) in two of its permutations — lying down between y = 0 and y = 1, and stood up between x = 0 and x = 1, where its floor is the semicircle that is also 𝒥*'s. A quadruple has up to eight permutations across the horizontal and vertical, four in each dual, so the same four numbers can sit in the arrangement more than one way.

The other two are not searched for but placed, by turning the strip inside out with the reflection from the inside-out lab: reflectQuad(strip, 2) is the classic (−1, 2, 2, 3), and reflecting that again gives (−3, 5, 8, 8). The reflection is exact integer arithmetic, so each lands where it belongs with nothing fitted.

An earlier version of this lab said the classic gasket was not contained, on a sample finding 111 of its 1,023 circles. That test placed it inside a unit circle at the origin — where the packings list keeps it, and nowhere near where it lives here. It measured a placement, not a containment. The classic gasket is in the arrangement; it just had to be asked in the right place.

Lowest terms divides that factor out. Since every bend is even, the whole arrangement is 2 × a primitive one — the same idea as a fraction reduced. Dividing the bends by 2 is exactly scaling the plane by 2, which gives a similar figure: same shape, twice the size, and therefore identical once the view refits. So only the numbers move, and they land on the workbench's own convention.

Per family colours each region with the circle it was cut out of. A circular region splits into three circles and four triangles; those triangles split into more triangles, so every triangle belongs to some circle however far back. Each circle takes a hue, its triangular fringe takes the same hue lighter, and the hue is derived from the circle itself — so stepping the generation grows the structure instead of repainting it. Half planes are circles of bend 0 and behave the same way.

Region counts follow (3·5ⁿ − 1)/2, splitting circular : triangular as 1:0, 3:4, 13:24, 63:124, 313:624. The triangular family grows faster and approaches twice the circular one.
