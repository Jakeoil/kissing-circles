Chapter 2

The jump

Three circles have two completions. Finding the first takes a square root. Finding the second, once you have the first, takes a subtraction — and that difference is the whole of what follows.

Soddy's relation is quadratic in each bend. Fix three of them and solve for the fourth:

b₄ = b₁ + b₂ + b₃ ± 2√(b₁b₂ + b₂b₃ + b₃b₁)

The two signs are the two circles. One drops into the gap between the three; the other wraps around the outside of them. Both genuinely touch all three, and the figure below shows them alternately — the three stay put, the fourth jumps.

The square root is avoidable

Look at the two roots in the panel and add them. The square root appears with a plus in one and a minus in the other, so it cancels:

b₄ + b₄′ = 2(b₁ + b₂ + b₃)

That is Vieta's relation between the roots of a quadratic and its coefficients, and it is the single most useful fact in this project. Rearranged:

b₄′ = 2(b₁ + b₂ + b₃) − b₄

Given one circle touching a triple, the other costs an addition and a subtraction. No square root. No division. If the four bends were whole numbers, the fifth is a whole number too — necessarily, not coincidentally.

Try the triple (−1, 2, 2). Its discriminant is zero, so the two roots coincide at 3 — but the two circles are still different, sitting above and below. The bends collide; the circles do not. Vieta still holds: 3 + 3 = 2 × 3.

The same trick moves the circle

A bend on its own does not place a circle. What makes this more than an arithmetic curiosity is that the identical relation holds for the positions. Writing each circle as its bend together with its bend times its center, (b, b·z), Lagarias, Mallows and Wilks showed the Descartes relation holds for those products too, with complex multiplication. Same quadratic, same Vieta, same subtraction:

(b·z)′ = 2(b₁z₁ + b₂z₂ + b₃z₃) − b₄z₄

So the whole circle — size and position — comes from subtracting. That single line is Circle.spawn in this project, and it is the entire recursion. Everything the workbench draws, to any depth, is that operation applied over and over.

Which is also why the arithmetic can stay exact forever. A method needing square roots would leave the integers on the first step and accumulate error on every one after. Subtraction does not. Chapter 3 is about what that buys.

What this means if you are typing curvatures

Three bends determine the fourth, to two values. Two bends determine nothing — a free parameter remains, and infinitely many circles complete them. And the four are an unordered collection in which repeats are ordinary: (−1, 2, 2, 3) has its 2 twice, and nothing distinguishes one from the other.

So the honest way to ask for a packing is to give three bends and be offered both completions — which is what the workbench's custom field should do, and currently does not.
