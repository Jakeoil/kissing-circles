Chapter 7

The arrangement

Chapter 6 gave two rules and one region. Apply both rules to every region, and keep going. What appears is bigger than the packing this project started with — and the packing is sitting inside it.

Schmidt's subdivision does not stop at one step. Every circular region splits into seven; every triangular region splits into four; each of those pieces is itself circular or triangular, and splits in turn. Nothing chooses between the rules. Both apply, everywhere, forever.

Draw the boundary circle of every region at every generation and you get the Schmidt arrangement of ℤ[i]. Schmidt built the subdivision in 1975; the name, and the modern reading of it, are Katherine Stange's — the arrangement is the orbit of the real line under the Bianchi group PSL(2, ℤ[i]).

Why outlines

Press filled. What comes back is wallpaper.

An Apollonian packing can be filled because its circles have disjoint interiors — nothing covers anything, so draw order does not matter and a fill is honest. The arrangement's circles nest. A region's boundary circle contains the boundary circles of every one of its descendants, so each generation paints over its own parent. What survives is only the last coat: the boundary between whichever circles happened to be drawn most recently.

The result is not blank — it is a handsome repeating pattern, which is the trap. It has the visual busyness of structure while carrying none of the actual structure. You cannot tell from it which circle contains which, and containment is the entire content of the arrangement. Outlines keep it because an outline does not hide what is behind it.

This is the same lesson as chapter 6 from the other side. There the mistake was superimposing two subdivisions that bound different regions; here it is using a drawing operation whose correctness depended on an assumption that has quietly stopped holding. Both were made in this project before they were understood.

The factor of two

Every curvature in the arrangement is even. Schmidt writes this as ρ(F) ∈ 2ℕ₀, and it is not a convention that could have gone the other way — it falls out of the construction. The first generation is the unit square with a circle of radius ½ at the midpoint of each side, so the smallest curvature that appears at all is 2.

So the strip packing (0, 0, 1, 1) appears here as (0, 0, 2, 2), and the classic packing (−1, 2, 2, 3) appears as (−2, 4, 4, 6). Same picture at half the size. Dividing every bend by 2 is exactly scaling the plane by 2, which is why the lab's lowest terms toggle changes the numbers and not the geometry.

The packing inside the arrangement

Press the gasket inside it. The lit circles are an ordinary Apollonian packing — the one on the workbench — and every one of them is a circle the arrangement had already drawn.

This is Stange's theorem, and what follows is a demonstration of it rather than a discovery. That Apollonian packings sit inside the Schmidt arrangement is the result her 2018 paper is built around. The figure that prompted this chapter — the (−6, 11, 14, 15) packing with its bends set in Computer Modern — is hers too.

What the program can add is the check. Searching the arrangement for four mutually tangent circles satisfying Descartes' theorem, allowing one to be re-oriented because a bounding circle must have negative bend, turns up (−2, 4, 4, 6) — and also (−4, 6, 12, 14), (−2, 4, 6, 12) and (0, 2, 2, 8), which are (−2, 3, 6, 7), (−1, 2, 3, 6) and (0, 1, 1, 4) doubled. Grow a packing from the first of these and compare it against the arrangement:

Of the gasket's circles with curvature up to 100, all 69 are present in the arrangement. Up to 200, 94 of 100 — and the six absent are exactly the ones the arrangement has not reached at this depth, not circles it lacks. Push the generation up and they arrive.

This is chapter 6's aside made concrete. The Apollonian move is the letter C applied to a triangular region. A packing is what you get by using that one letter and discarding 𝒱₁ 𝒱₂ 𝒱₃ ℰ₁ ℰ₂ ℰ₃ — one letter's worth of a seven-letter alphabet. Every circle of the packing is therefore a circle of the arrangement, reached by a word that happens to use no other letter.

The packing was never the whole object. It is one orbit inside a larger one, and the larger one is where the arithmetic lives: the arrangement is indexed by ℤ[i], and chapter 8 asks what the same construction looks like one dimension down, over ℤ.

On being able to draw this at all. Until recently this figure stopped at generation 6, because the subdivision expands 5n regions whether or not they are anywhere near the picture. Pruning it needs a bound on everything a region's subtree can reach, and the one that works is that subdivision is a partition: a region's descendants lie inside it, so one exact bounding box bounds a whole subtree's position and its size at once.
